What to do after Class 10

After Class 10 there are three roads: carry on to Class 11 and 12, learn a skill through a diploma or ITI, or do both in turn. None of them closes the others for ever.

Road 1: Class 11 and 12

This keeps the most doors open, because almost every degree and many jobs ask for Class 12. You choose a stream:

  • Science with Mathematics (PCM): needed for engineering, architecture, the technical wings of the defence forces, and useful for computers, economics and statistics.
  • Science with Biology (PCB): needed for medicine, dentistry, nursing, pharmacy, agriculture and the life sciences. Taking both Mathematics and Biology keeps both sets open, at the cost of a heavier load.
  • Commerce: the natural base for B.Com, chartered accountancy, company secretary, banking and business study. Mathematics as an optional subject helps later.
  • Arts or Humanities: history, political science, economics, psychology, languages and more. Open to law, civil services, design, journalism, teaching and social work.

Science students can move to commerce or arts courses after 12th. The reverse is mostly not possible. That is a reason to take Science only if you can manage it, not a reason to take it by default.

Road 2: a skill course

A three year polytechnic diploma makes you a junior engineer and lets you enter the second year of B.Tech later. An ITI trade takes one or two years and leads to work as an electrician, fitter, mechanic, welder or operator, often through a paid apprenticeship. See skilled trades.

Road 3: work now, study alongside

If the family needs you to earn, you can work and still complete Class 12 through the National Institute of Open Schooling (NIOS) or your state's open school. Government posts open at this level include multi-tasking staff and constable posts recruited by SSC, and Group D posts in the railways.

How to choose a stream

  1. Look at your Class 9 and 10 marks subject by subject, and at which subjects you study without being pushed.
  2. List two or three careers that interest you and note which stream each needs.
  3. Talk to someone who works in that field, not only to relatives with opinions.
  4. Check which subjects your school actually offers, and how well it teaches them.

Do not pick a stream because friends did, or because it is thought to be "for toppers". Two years of a subject you dislike usually ends in poor marks and a change of plan anyway.

Questions people ask

Which stream is best after 10th?

There is no best stream. Science keeps the most courses open but is the heaviest; commerce and arts lead to equally good careers. Choose by the subjects you can study steadily and the careers that interest you.

Can I do a diploma after 10th and still get a degree?

Yes. After a three year polytechnic diploma you can join the second year of B.Tech by lateral entry.
