Resume summary and career objective, with examples

These three lines are the only part of your resume that everyone reads. Spend more time on them than on anything else.

Summary or objective?

A career objective looks forward: what you have learned and the work you want. It suits freshers and people changing field. A summary looks back: who you are professionally and your best results. It suits anyone with a work record. Use one, not both.

A simple pattern

Fresher: [qualification] with [practical exposure or strongest skill]. [One fact that shows it.] Looking for [role] where I can [what you will do].

Experienced: [Role] with [years] in [field or industry]. [Scale of work.] [Best result with a number.] Looking for [next role].

Keep it to two to four lines. Use facts. Remove every adjective you cannot prove: hardworking, sincere, dynamic, passionate, result-oriented.

Weak

To work in a challenging environment in a reputed organisation where I can utilise my skills and knowledge for the growth of the company and myself.

Better

B.Sc. Computer Science graduate who has built two small web applications in Python and Django. Comfortable with SQL and Git. Looking for a junior developer or support engineer role.

Career objectives for freshers

  • Accounts: Commerce graduate trained in Tally and GST return preparation during a two month internship at a CA firm. Careful with figures and comfortable in Excel. Seeking an accounts assistant role.
  • Software: B.Tech computer science student with two deployed web projects and a strong base in Java and data structures. Looking for a software developer trainee position.
  • Mechanical: Diploma mechanical engineer with six weeks of in-plant training on CNC turning and preventive maintenance. Can read drawings and use measuring instruments. Ready for shift work.
  • Civil: B.E. civil graduate with site training on a residential project, where I assisted in levelling, bar bending schedules and daily progress reports. Seeking a site engineer trainee role.
  • Electrical: Diploma electrical engineer with hands-on practice in panel wiring and motor testing from a three week industrial training. Looking for a maintenance trainee position.
  • Marketing: MBA marketing student who visited 120 retail outlets and added 18 new ones during a summer internship. Enjoy field work and data. Seeking a management trainee role in sales.
  • Finance: MBA finance student with a project on working capital of a mid-sized firm and good command of Excel. Looking for an analyst or credit operations trainee role.
  • HR: MBA HR student who screened 200 resumes and scheduled 60 interviews as an intern. Organised and good at follow-up. Seeking an HR trainee or recruiter role.
  • Customer support: Arts graduate with clear spoken English and Hindi, typing speed of 40 words a minute and part-time experience at a retail counter. Comfortable with rotational shifts.
  • Teaching: B.Ed. graduate with 40 lessons of teaching practice in Classes 6 to 8 science. Use models and experiments to explain concepts. Seeking a TGT science post.
  • Pharmacy: B.Pharm graduate with one month of hospital pharmacy training in dispensing and stock records. Registered pharmacist. Looking for a pharmacist or medical representative role.
  • Office work after Class 12: Class 12 pass with a six month computer course and typing speed of 35 words a minute. One year of part-time work in a transport office. Looking for a data entry or office assistant job.
  • Graphic design: Diploma in graphic design with a portfolio of 15 logos, posters and social media sets made for local businesses and college events. Looking for a junior designer role.
  • Hotel management: Hotel management graduate with six months of industrial training in front office and food service at a business hotel. Seeking a guest service associate role.

Summaries for experienced candidates

  • Accountant: Accountant with five years in trading and manufacturing firms, handling books up to finalisation for a turnover of about ₹40 crore. File GST and TDS returns independently and coordinate the audit.
  • Sales: Field sales executive with three years in FMCG distribution, covering 180 outlets. Met or beat target in 26 of the last 30 months and opened 45 new outlets.
  • Developer: Back-end developer with four years in Java and Spring Boot. Own two payment services end to end and cut a key API's response time by 60%.
  • Teacher: Science teacher with three years of experience in Classes 6 to 10 at a CBSE school. Full pass results in Class 10 for two years; coordinate the science exhibition.
  • HR: HR executive with two years in recruitment for a 300 person company. Closed 85 positions last year with an average time to fill of 24 days.
  • Store manager: Retail store manager with six years of experience, leading a team of 14. Raised the store from seventh to second in the region on sales per square foot within a year.
  • Production supervisor: Production supervisor with seven years on injection moulding lines. Manage a shift of 22 operators; reduced rejection from 4% to 1.5% through mould maintenance checks.
  • Digital marketer: Digital marketing executive with three years managing search and social campaigns for education clients. Lowered cost per enquiry by about a third over twelve months.
  • Nurse: Staff nurse with four years in a 200 bed hospital, the last two in the intensive care unit. Registered with the State Nursing Council; trained six new joiners.
  • Returning after a break: Accounts executive with four years of experience before a three year career break for family care. Updated my skills with a GST practice course in 2026. Ready to return full time.

Changing it for each job

Read the advertisement and bring its main words into your lines, where they are true of you. If the post asks for "TDS and GST", say TDS and GST, not "taxation". If it asks for "inside sales", do not headline yourself as field sales. This small change, done honestly, is the difference between a resume that looks sent to everyone and one that looks written for this employer.

Questions people ask

How long should a resume summary be?

Two to four lines, about 40 to 70 words. It should fit comfortably above the first section heading.

Can I copy an objective from the internet?

Use examples to learn the pattern, then write your own with your real facts. Recruiters recognise copied lines at once.

Should I use "I" in the summary?

You may leave the pronoun out ("Accountant with five years...") or use "I" sparingly. Keep the style the same throughout.
