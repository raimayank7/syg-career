What a good resume does

A resume has one purpose: to get you a call for an interview. The person reading it is busy, has many others to read, and gives yours a short first look. In that look they want to see three things. What are you? What can you do? Is there proof? A resume that answers those questions in the top half of one page gets read further. One that opens with a copied objective and eight lines of personal particulars usually does not.

So the rules are few. Put the most relevant thing first. Write short points that begin with what you did and say how much. Be specific about skills. Keep it to one page. Tell the truth. Everything on this site is an application of those five rules.

For freshers

You have more to write than you think. An internship, a project, a part-time job, help in a family shop, a position in a college club: each is experience when described by what you did. Start with the fresher resume guide, or open the B.Com, B.Tech or after Class 12 sample and make it yours.

For job changers

Your record is your case. Lead with results and the scale of your work, cut what is old or unrelated, and keep confidential details out. The guide for experienced people shows how, and gaps and job changes covers breaks, short stints and a change of field.
