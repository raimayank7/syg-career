Resume samples

11 one-page samples for common jobs in India, for freshers and for people with experience. Each has notes on why it works and opens in the builder with one tap.

  • B.Com fresher resume sample (accounts assistant) For commerce graduates applying for a first job as an accounts assistant, junior accountant, billing executive or article assistant.
  • B.Tech computer science fresher resume sample For engineering freshers applying for software developer, trainee engineer or QA roles, through campus placement or off campus.
  • MBA marketing fresher resume sample For management graduates applying for marketing, sales, brand and business development trainee roles.
  • Diploma mechanical engineering fresher resume sample For polytechnic diploma holders applying for diploma engineer trainee, production, quality or maintenance roles in manufacturing units.
  • Customer support (BPO) fresher resume sample For graduates and undergraduates of any stream applying for voice, chat or email support and back-office roles.
  • Resume sample after Class 12 (office assistant, data entry) For candidates who have completed Class 12, or are studying by distance mode, and want office assistant, data entry operator, receptionist or store helper jobs.
  • Sales executive resume sample (3 years of experience) For field sales, area sales and business development executives looking for a better role.
  • Accountant resume sample (5 years of experience) For accountants, senior accounts executives and accounts officers changing jobs.
  • Software developer resume sample (4 years of experience) For developers changing jobs after three to six years.
  • School teacher resume sample For primary, TGT and PGT teachers applying to private schools.
  • HR executive resume sample (2 years of experience) For HR executives and recruiters early in their careers.

How to use a sample

A sample is a pattern, not a text to copy. The facts in it belong to an invented person. What you can take is the shape: the order of sections, the way each point begins with an action and carries a number, how much is said and how much is left out. Choose the sample nearest to your own situation, open it in the builder, and replace it line by line with your own facts. Where the sample has a number, look for the matching number in your own work. Where it has an achievement you do not have, delete the line; do not invent one.

  1. Pick the closest sample: by stage first (fresher or experienced), then by kind of work.
  2. Read the notes under it: why it is written this way, what to change, what to avoid.
  3. Tap "Open this sample in the builder".
  4. Change the name and contact details first, so you never send a page with sample details on it.
  5. Rewrite the summary last, when you can see the whole page.

Why every sample is one page

For freshers and for most people with under ten years of work, one page is enough and is easier for a recruiter to take in. The samples show that a full record, with numbers, fits comfortably when each line is chosen. If yours runs over, the guide on fitting a resume on one page shows what to cut first.

What the samples have in common

  • A headline under the name that says what the person is, in a few words.
  • Two to four lines of summary or objective made of facts, not adjectives.
  • Points that begin with an action word and include how many or how much.
  • Skills in labelled groups, with tools named.
  • No photograph, date of birth, father's name or declaration, because private employers rarely need them. See personal details.

If your job is not here

The pattern carries over to any work. A nurse, an electrician, a driver, a chef and a bank clerk can all write a headline, a factual summary, points with numbers and a grouped skill list. Start from the sample closest in stage, and use the skills and action words guides to find your own words. For a longer academic CV or a biodata, see career.shareyourgroup.com/cv.

General guidance. This page gives general suggestions on writing a resume, reviewed in November 2026. Employers differ: if an advertisement or application form asks for a particular format or detail, follow it. Every name, employer and college in our examples is invented. Nothing here promises an interview or a job.
