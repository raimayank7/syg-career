Job portal and professional profile: how to be found

On a job portal, recruiters do not read resumes one by one. They search. Your profile fields decide whether you appear in the search.

How recruiters use portals

A recruiter with a vacancy types in a job title, a few skills, a location and a range of experience, and gets a list of profiles. They scan the list (headline, current role, key skills, location, last active date) and open the ones that look right. Only then is your uploaded resume read. So the profile has two jobs: to match the search, and to earn the click.

Fill every field

Incomplete profiles are easy to pass over, and many search filters work on fields you may have left blank. Go through the whole form once, carefully.

  • Headline. The most-read line. Use your role, main skills and years: "Accountant | GST, TDS, Finalisation | 5 years". Not "Looking for a job" or "Seeking opportunities".
  • Key skills. The words recruiters search. Enter each skill separately, in the common spelling, as on the skills page. Include tools by name.
  • Current and preferred location. If you would move, add those cities to preferred locations.
  • Experience and designation. Use the common name for your role. Exact months matter; they should match your resume.
  • Summary. The same facts as your resume summary, in three or four lines.
  • Education and certifications, with years.
  • Notice period or availability, where there is a field. Recruiters filter on it.
  • Resume file. Upload the current text PDF. See ATS-friendly resume.

Keep the profile and the resume in step

Dates, titles, employers and qualifications must be the same in both. A recruiter who sees "3 years" on the profile and two on the resume will not call to ask which is right.

Stay active

Portals generally show recruiters when a candidate was last active, and searches can be sorted by it. Log in regularly while you are searching, and update something small when your details change: a new skill, a new certificate, a revised summary. Do not make meaningless edits just to appear active; improve the profile instead.

Apply well, not widely

  • Read the description and apply where you meet most of what is asked.
  • Use the portal's filters for location, experience and date posted.
  • Set job alerts for your title and city so that you see postings early.
  • Keep a list of where you applied, with dates.

A professional networking profile

On networking sites the same rules apply, with a few additions.

  • Photo: here a clear, friendly head-and-shoulders photo is normal and helps people recognise you.
  • Headline: role and strengths, not just "Student at...".
  • About: a short paragraph in the first person: what you do, what you are good at, what you are looking for.
  • Experience: two or three of your best points per role.
  • Connections: classmates, teachers, colleagues, people you have actually met. When you ask a stranger to connect, add a one-line note saying why.
  • Activity: a thoughtful comment or an occasional post about your work or learning does more than a hundred "interested" replies under job posts.

Privacy if you are employed

If you do not want your current employer to know you are looking, check the portal's privacy settings. Many let you hide your profile from named companies or keep it visible only when you apply. Do not use your office email or phone, and do not browse or reply during office hours on office systems.

Stay safe from job fraud

Job seekers are a target for fraud. Protect yourself with a few fixed rules.

  • Never pay for a job. Genuine employers do not charge candidates a registration fee, security deposit, training fee or "processing charge" for an offer.
  • Be wary of offers without an interview, or with pay far above the normal for the work.
  • Check the sender. Offer letters from free email addresses, with spelling mistakes or pressure to act today, deserve suspicion. Find the company's official website yourself and confirm through the contact details there.
  • Do not share one-time passwords, bank PINs or card details with anyone, and do not install apps that a "recruiter" sends.
  • Give identity documents only at the proper stage, through official channels, after you are satisfied the employer is real.
  • For jobs abroad, deal only with properly authorised recruiting agents and verify the offer independently.

If you have been cheated, report it to the police without delay, and tell your bank at once if money has left your account. Keep the messages, numbers and payment references as evidence.

One week plan

  1. Day 1: finish your resume in the builder.
  2. Day 2: complete your profile on one major portal, field by field.
  3. Day 3: set alerts; apply to five well-matched jobs with a short message each.
  4. Day 4: complete a networking profile; connect with twenty people you know.
  5. Day 5 to 7: apply to five more a day; note each application; prepare for calls with the checklist beside you.

Questions people ask

What should I write in a job portal headline?

Your role, two or three key skills and years of experience, for example "Accountant | GST, TDS, Finalisation | 5 years".

How often should I update my profile?

Log in regularly while you are job hunting and update it whenever your skills, role or availability change.

Do genuine employers ask candidates to pay?

No. Treat any demand for a registration, training or security fee in return for a job as a warning sign.
