Skills section of a resume, with examples

A skill on a resume is a promise that you can do something if asked. List the promises you can keep, and say how well.

Hard skills first

Hard skills are things you can be tested on: software, machines, languages, methods, typing speed. Soft skills are ways of working: communication, teamwork, patience. Recruiters and resume-search software look for hard skills by name. Soft skills are better shown through your experience points than listed. "Resolved about 20 customer complaints a week" proves communication and patience; the bare words prove nothing.

Four rules

  1. Group them. Three or four groups with a label each are easier to read than one long line. In the builder, write one group per line, with the label before a colon.
  2. Be specific. Not "computer knowledge" but "MS Excel (VLOOKUP, pivot tables), MS Word, email". Not "accounting" but "bank reconciliation, GST returns, TDS".
  3. Show the level where it varies. "Java (strong), Python (basic)". "English (fluent), Tamil (mother tongue), Hindi (conversational)".
  4. Match the advertisement. If it asks for a skill you have, use the same words it uses. Do not add a skill you lack because the advertisement mentions it.
Weak

Skills: Computer, Communication, Hardworking, Team player, MS Office, Internet, Positive attitude

Better

Accounting: Tally (vouchers, GST reports), bank reconciliation
Computer: MS Excel (SUMIF, VLOOKUP, pivot tables), MS Word
Other: English typing 35 wpm

These are prompts to help you remember what you can do, not lists to copy. Include a skill only if it is true of you.

How many skills

Eight to fifteen items in three or four groups suit most resumes. A list of thirty looks like a list of none. If you have many, keep those the target job needs and those you are best at.

Languages

List the languages you can work in, with level if they differ. In many jobs in India (sales, support, teaching, healthcare, field work) a regional language is a real qualification. Do not claim one you can only understand.

Certificates

A certificate supports a skill but does not replace showing it. Put the skill in the skills section and the certificate, with year, under achievements. Leave out certificates unrelated to the job and those from very short courses, unless you have nothing else.

Skills you are still learning

It is fine to list them, marked honestly: "Power BI (learning, course in progress)". Employers hiring juniors often value the fact that you are learning. What damages a candidate is claiming mastery and failing the first question.

Where the skills section goes

After experience or projects for most people. For technical roles where tools decide the shortlist, it can go just under the summary. Either way, the same skills should also appear inside your experience points, which is where a reader believes them.

Questions people ask

How many skills should I list on a resume?

About eight to fifteen, in three or four labelled groups. Choose the ones the job needs and you are strongest in.

Should I list soft skills such as teamwork?

It is better to show them through experience points with facts. A bare list of soft skills adds little.

Can I list a skill I am still learning?

Yes, if you mark it honestly, for example "learning" or "basic".
