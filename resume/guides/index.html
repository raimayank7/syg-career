Resume writing guides

17 guides, each on one question, written for job seekers in India. Read the one you need, then put it to work in the builder.

  • How to write a resume, step by step A resume has one job: to get you a call. It does that by letting a busy stranger see, in half a minute, that you fit the work.
  • Fresher resume format: what to write when you have no experience You have more to write than you think. Experience is not only paid work: it is anything you did that shows you can do the job.
  • Resume for experienced people changing jobs With a work record, your resume stops being about potential and becomes a record of results. Lead with them.
  • Resume summary and career objective, with examples These three lines are the only part of your resume that everyone reads. Spend more time on them than on anything else.
  • How to write work experience points Most resumes describe a job. Good ones describe what the person did in it. The difference is in how each point is built.
  • Skills section of a resume, with examples A skill on a resume is a promise that you can do something if asked. List the promises you can keep, and say how well.
  • How to write projects and internships in a resume For a fresher, the project section does the work that job history does for others. Write it as work, because it was.
  • How to write the education section Education is a short section. Get its facts exactly right and give it the right amount of space for your stage.
  • How to fit your resume on one page One page is not a rule for its own sake. It is a way of forcing you to choose your best material, which is exactly what the reader wants.
  • ATS-friendly resume: a format software can read Before a person reads your resume, a program may have read it first. Write so that neither of them has to guess.
  • Twenty resume mistakes and how to fix them Most resumes are rejected for small, avoidable reasons. Go through this list with your own page in front of you.
  • Resume, CV and biodata: the difference In India the three words are used as if they meant the same thing. They do not, and sending the wrong one is a small but real mistake.
  • Email and cover letter for sending your resume An attachment with no message is easy to ignore. Four clear lines above it can decide whether the resume is opened.
  • Career gaps, short jobs and changing field A gap explained in one plain line worries an employer far less than a gap left blank. Honesty, briefly put, is the best wording.
  • Photo, declaration and personal details: what an Indian resume needs Most of what tradition puts at the bottom of an Indian resume is not needed by a private employer. Include a personal detail when it is asked for, not by habit.
  • Job portal and professional profile: how to be found On a job portal, recruiters do not read resumes one by one. They search. Your profile fields decide whether you appear in the search.
  • Action words for resume points The first word of each point sets its strength. "Responsible for" says you had a duty. "Reduced" says you did something.

Which guide to read first

If you are starting from a blank page, begin with how to write a resume. It gives the whole shape in ten minutes: which sections, in what order, and how to write each line. Then read the guide for your situation: fresher resume if you have not worked yet, or resume for experienced people if you are changing jobs.

After that, improve one section at a time. The three lines at the top matter most, so spend time on summary and objective. The body of the page is made of points, and experience points shows how to build each one from an action, a number and a result, with the action words list beside it. Skills, projects and internships and education each have their own page.

Questions particular to India

Some questions come up here more than anywhere else. Should the resume carry a photograph, date of birth, father's name and a signed declaration? Is the document a resume, a CV or a biodata? How should years spent preparing for competitive examinations be shown? These are answered plainly in photo, declaration and personal details, resume, CV and biodata and gaps and job changes.

Before you send

Three guides deal with the last step. One page resume shows what to cut and how to tighten lines. ATS-friendly resume explains how to keep the page readable by the software many employers use. Cover letter and email gives the few lines to write when you send it. Then run through the final checklist, and keep common mistakes open in another tab.

Learn from samples

Reading advice is one thing; seeing it applied is another. Each of our sample resumes comes with notes on why it is written as it is and what to change for yourself, and each can be opened in the builder with one tap.

General guidance. This page gives general suggestions on writing a resume, reviewed in November 2026. Employers differ: if an advertisement or application form asks for a particular format or detail, follow it. Every name, employer and college in our examples is invented. Nothing here promises an interview or a job.
