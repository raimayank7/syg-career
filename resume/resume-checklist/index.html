Resume checklist before you send

24 checks in five groups. Open your resume beside this page and tick each line only when you have actually looked.

Why a checklist

By the time a resume is finished, its writer has read it so often that the eye slides over it. That is when a wrong digit in the phone number or a missing attachment slips through, and those small slips cost more interviews than weak wording ever does. A checklist makes you look at each thing separately. Pilots and surgeons use them for the same reason: not because the steps are hard, but because they are easy to skip.

The first group matters most. If the phone number or email is wrong, nothing else on the page can help you. Do those two tests, a call and a test mail, every single time you change the file.

After you send

Keep your phone charged and reachable, and answer unknown numbers politely during working hours; recruiters often call without notice. Keep a copy of the exact resume you sent to each employer, since you may be asked about it weeks later. And keep the list of applications up to date, so that when a call comes you know at once which post it is about.

Questions people ask

How long does this check take?

About ten minutes with your resume open beside it. It is worth doing before every application, because details such as the file and the message change each time.

Are my ticks saved?

No. The list starts fresh each time you open the page, so that you check again for each application.
