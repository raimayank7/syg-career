Engineering: the path in India

Engineering is the most common professional course in India. That makes it easy to enter and harder to stand out in.

What the work is

Engineers design, build, test and maintain things: software, machines, roads, power systems, chips. Day to day, most engineering work is problem solving in a team, with a computer.

The path, step by step

  1. Class 11 and 12 in Science with Physics, Chemistry and Mathematics (PCM).
  2. Entrance exam: JEE Main for NITs, IIITs and many other colleges; JEE Advanced for IITs; your state's exam or 12th marks for state colleges.
  3. B.Tech or B.E., four years, in a branch such as computer science, electronics, mechanical, civil or electrical.
  4. Then a job through campus placement or your own search, or higher study: M.Tech through GATE, an MBA, or study abroad.

Examinations and bodies

Examination or bodyWhat it is for
JEE MainNational exam for NITs, IIITs and other colleges. It is also the first stage for the IITs.
JEE AdvancedFor admission to the IITs. Only those who clear the JEE Main cut-off may sit it, and only in two consecutive years.
State entrance examsMany states hold their own exam or admit on 12th marks for colleges in the state.
GATEAfter B.Tech, for M.Tech admission and for recruitment by several public sector companies.

How long it takes

Four years after 12th. Six years after 10th by the diploma route (three years of polytechnic, then entry into the second year of B.Tech).

Honest notes

  • The college and the skills you build matter more than the word "engineer" on the degree. A degree from a weak college with no skills does not get a job by itself.
  • Do not choose a branch only because it is in fashion. Look at what you enjoy doing; branch trends change within four years.
  • Coding can be learned in any branch, and many engineers from all branches work in software.
  • The diploma route is slower but cheaper, and you can earn after the diploma.

Where to check

  • jeemain.nta.nic.in for JEE Main
  • jeeadv.ac.in for JEE Advanced
  • The AICTE website for approved colleges
  • Your state's technical education department for state counselling
