Taking a drop year: how to decide, and how to use it

A year spent only on one examination can be the best decision you make or a year you regret. The difference usually lies in why you take it and how you plan it.

First, why did this attempt fall short?

Be exact. There are only a few possible reasons, and each points a different way:

  • The syllabus was not finished. A second year can fix this, if you know why it happened.
  • You knew the material but lost marks in the paper. That is a practice and nerves problem, also fixable.
  • Illness or a family event. A fair reason to try again.
  • You did not really study. A year at home, with less structure than school, rarely changes this by itself.
  • You gave it everything and were still far away. This is the hardest one to hear. A second year of the same effort tends to bring a similar result.

Questions before you decide

  1. Is this the course I want, or the one I was told to want? A drop year for someone else's dream is a heavy thing to carry.
  2. What seat do I have in hand now? Compare it honestly with what a better result could bring. Sometimes the seat in hand is good enough.
  3. Does the examination allow another attempt? Read the current rules on attempts and age on the official website. They differ by examination and they change.
  4. Can my family afford the year? Not only coaching, but a year in which you are not moving ahead.
  5. How do I do alone? Friends will be in college. Some people study well alone; some sink.

If you take it: planning the year

  • Start within two weeks. A long break after results turns into a lost summer.
  • Work from the official syllabus and past papers. List the chapters that cost you marks and begin there, not at chapter one.
  • Fix a daily timetable with a stop time. Regular hours beat heroic nights. Keep one half day a week free.
  • Test yourself every week under timed conditions, and spend as long studying your mistakes as you spent on the test.
  • Look after your body and mind. Sleep, some exercise, and people to talk to. If you feel low for weeks, tell someone at home and see a doctor or counsellor. An examination is never worth your health.

Keep a second road open

Apply for the fallback as well as the dream. Fill in the forms for other examinations and for universities that admit on Class 12 marks or a common test. Some students enrol in a degree by distance or open learning during the year, so that the year counts for something whatever the result. Check the rules of your target examination before doing this.

When to stop

Decide the limit before the year begins, and tell your family. For most school leavers one extra year is a reasonable limit, and a second needs a very clear reason. For long examinations taken after a degree, people often set a number of attempts or an age at which they will move on. Stopping is not failing. The reading, the discipline and the habit of hard work go with you into whatever comes next.

A word to parents

A child in a drop year already feels the pressure. Daily questions about marks do not add study hours; they take away sleep. Agree on the plan and the limit together, then let the plan work.

Questions people ask

Is a drop year a bad mark on my record?

Colleges that admit through an entrance examination look at the rank, not at the year you passed Class 12. Later, employers may ask about a gap, and "I prepared for an examination" is an honest and common answer.

Should I join coaching for the drop year?

Only if you know what it will add. Coaching gives structure and tests. If your problem was unfinished syllabus or lack of practice it can help; if you studied well and want quiet, self-study with test series may serve better.

Can I do a degree and prepare at the same time?

Many do, especially for examinations taken after graduation. For JEE and NEET the load is heavy, so students often choose a distance course or a lighter one. Check that your target examination allows it.

How many attempts are allowed?

It differs by examination and the rules change. Read the current information bulletin on the official website of the body that holds it.
