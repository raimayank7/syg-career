What to do after graduation

After a degree the choice is between working, studying further, and preparing for a competitive examination. Many people combine two of these.

Government examinations

  • UPSC Civil Services and state PSC examinations.
  • SSC CGL: for inspectors, assistants and auditors in central departments.
  • Banks: IBPS and SBI for officers and clerks.
  • Railways: RRB NTPC graduate posts.
  • Defence: CDS and AFCAT.
  • Teaching: B.Ed and then CTET or TET.

Any degree qualifies for most of these. Check the age limit of each: they differ, and they decide how many years you have.

Further study

  • MBA or PGDM: through CAT for the IIMs, and XAT, CMAT and other tests elsewhere.
  • Master's degree in your subject: through CUET PG for central universities and others. For engineers, M.Tech through GATE.
  • Three year LLB, open to graduates of any subject.
  • B.Ed, two years, to teach in schools.
  • UGC NET after a master's, for college teaching and research fellowships.

Work

A first job teaches things no course does: deadlines, colleagues, customers. If you are unsure what to study next, a year or two of work usually makes the answer clearer, and many postgraduate courses value experience.

Preparing full time: set a limit

Full-time preparation for government examinations is common. Set a limit before you begin, in years or in attempts, and keep one skill or qualification growing on the side. A gap of several years with nothing to show is hard to explain later.

Questions people ask

Which government examinations can any graduate take?

UPSC Civil Services, state PSC examinations, SSC CGL, bank examinations of IBPS and SBI, RRB NTPC graduate posts and CDS are open to graduates of any subject, within their age limits.

Is an MBA necessary?

No. It helps for management roles, most of all from a good institute and after some work experience. It is not required for a career in business.
