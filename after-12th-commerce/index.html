What to do after Class 12 Commerce

Commerce leads to two kinds of course: a degree, and a professional qualification. Many students do one of each at the same time.

Degrees

  • B.Com: three years (four with honours in many universities). The general base for accounts, tax, banking and business.
  • BBA or BMS: management study, often with internships. Admission by the university's test, CUET or marks.
  • BA or B.Sc Economics: for those who like the analytical side. Mathematics in Class 12 is often required.
  • Integrated law: BBA LLB or B.Com LLB, five years, through CLAT and other tests.
  • Five year integrated management at some IIMs, through their aptitude test.

Professional courses

  • Chartered accountancy (CA): audit, tax and finance.
  • Company secretary (CS): company law and governance.
  • Cost and management accountancy (CMA).

All three are run by institutes set up by Acts of Parliament, charge modest fees and can be done alongside a degree. All three have low pass rates and demand years of steady work.

Jobs and exams later

After graduation, commerce students do well in bank examinations, SSC CGL (which fills many accounts and tax posts), insurance, and the civil services. An MBA after a degree, through CAT and similar tests, is the usual route to management jobs.

How to choose

If you enjoy accounts and can study on your own for long stretches, try CA or CMA. If you prefer people and organising, look at BBA and management. If you are unsure, a B.Com from a good college keeps every one of these open while you find out.

Questions people ask

Can I do CA with B.Com?

Yes, and most students do. The CA course has its own classes and examinations, so many take B.Com alongside by regular or distance mode.

Is Mathematics needed for commerce courses?

Not for B.Com or CA. It is often required for economics honours and helps in management entrance tests.
