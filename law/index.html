How to become a lawyer in India

You can study law straight after school or after any degree. Both routes end at the same place: enrolment as an advocate.

What the work is

Lawyers advise people and organisations on their rights and duties, draft documents and argue cases. Many never go to court: they work in companies, law firms, banks, government and the judiciary.

The path, step by step

  1. Route one, after 12th in any stream: a five year integrated course such as BA LLB or BBA LLB. Admission to the National Law Universities is through CLAT; other universities use their own tests or CUET.
  2. Route two, after graduation in any subject: a three year LLB.
  3. Enrol with a State Bar Council after the degree.
  4. Pass the All India Bar Examination to get the certificate of practice.
  5. For the judiciary, states hold their own judicial service examinations for law graduates.

Examinations and bodies

Examination or bodyWhat it is for
CLATCommon Law Admission Test, for the National Law Universities.
AILETThe separate test of National Law University, Delhi.
All India Bar ExaminationHeld by the Bar Council of India for law graduates who wish to practise.
State judicial service examsFor appointment as a civil judge.

How long it takes

Five years after 12th, or three years after a degree.

Honest notes

  • The early years of court practice usually pay little. Income grows with reputation, slowly.
  • Law school is reading and writing, a lot of both, in English. If you dislike reading, think again.
  • Check that the college is approved by the Bar Council of India. Without that, you cannot enrol as an advocate.
  • A law degree is also a good base for civil services, company secretary work and policy jobs.

Is this path for you?

It may suit you if:

  • You like reading closely and arguing a point with reasons.
  • You write clearly, or are willing to work at it.
  • You can wait: the first years in practice are slow.

Think again if:

  • You expect court work to look like it does in films.
  • You dislike reading long documents.

If the main road does not open

If the national law universities are out of reach, a law degree from a good state university or college leads to the same enrolment as an advocate. The three year LLB after any degree keeps the door open for years, so a student can study another subject first and still enter law later.

Where to check

  • consortiumofnlus.ac.in for CLAT
  • The Bar Council of India website for approved colleges and the bar examination

Questions people ask

How long does it take?

Five years after 12th, or three years after a degree.

Which examination do I need?

CLAT: Common Law Admission Test, for the National Law Universities. AILET: The separate test of National Law University, Delhi. All India Bar Examination: Held by the Bar Council of India for law graduates who wish to practise.
