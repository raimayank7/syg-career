"What is your biggest weakness?" How to answer

A question asked in almost every interview. Why it is asked, how to build your answer, model answers, and what to avoid.

Why interviewers ask it

Nobody expects a candidate without weaknesses. The interviewer is checking honesty and self-awareness, and whether you work on your faults. A disguised boast ("I work too hard") fails on both counts.

How to build your answer

  1. Choose a real weakness that is not central to the job. An accountant should not choose "careless with numbers".
  2. Say it plainly in one sentence.
  3. Describe what you are doing about it, with something concrete.
  4. Mention the progress so far, honestly.
  5. Stop. Do not list three more.

Model answers

Fresher

I get nervous speaking in front of a group. In my first college presentation I rushed through it. Since then I have volunteered to present in every project, and last semester I joined the debate club to force myself to practise. I am still not a natural speaker, but I can now present without reading from the slides.

Experienced

I used to take on too much myself instead of delegating, because it felt quicker. Last year it caught up with me when two deadlines fell together. Since then I list the tasks each Monday and decide which ones a junior can do, and I spend time showing them properly. It has made the team stronger and my own work better, though I still have to stop myself now and then.

Fresher (technical gap)

My spoken English is not as fluent as I would like, since I studied in Hindi medium until Class 12. I read an English newspaper every day and have been practising with a speaking group for three months. I can now handle a conversation like this one, and I am continuing to work on it.

These are examples about invented people. Keep the structure, and put in your own facts and numbers.

What to avoid

  • "I have no weaknesses."
  • A fake weakness: "I am a perfectionist", "I care too much".
  • A weakness that disqualifies you for the job.
  • A personal confession that has nothing to do with work.
  • A weakness with no sign of effort to improve.

Likely follow-up questions

  • How has that affected your work? Give one small honest instance and what you changed.
  • What would you like to learn next? Name a skill useful in this job.
