"Can you explain the gap in your career?" How to answer

A question asked in almost every interview. Why it is asked, how to build your answer, model answers, and what to avoid.

Why interviewers ask it

A gap makes an interviewer wonder what happened and whether you are ready to work now. A calm, truthful answer in two or three sentences usually settles it. Hesitation or a changing story is what causes doubt.

How to build your answer

  1. State what happened, plainly, in one sentence.
  2. Say what you did in that time that has any value: study, courses, caring, part-time work.
  3. Say why you are ready now.
  4. Do not apologise or over-explain. Then stop and let them ask the next question.

Model answers

Competitive exam preparation

After graduation I prepared full time for banking and state service examinations for two years. I cleared one preliminary stage but not the mains. I have decided to build my career in accounts, and in the last six months I completed a Tally and GST course. The preparation did improve my quantitative and writing skills, which I use every day.

Family care

I left my job in 2023 to look after my mother during her treatment. She is well now, and other family members are close by. I kept my skills current with an advanced Excel course this year and am ready to return full time.

Maternity and childcare

I took a break of three years after my daughter was born. She has started school, and I have arranged reliable care for the afternoons. Before the break I worked for four years as an HR executive, and I have refreshed my knowledge with a course on labour law basics.

Long job search

After my contract ended, it took longer than I expected to find the right role. In that time I did freelance bookkeeping for two traders and learned an accounting package I had not used before, so the months were not wasted.

These are examples about invented people. Keep the structure, and put in your own facts and numbers.

What to avoid

  • Hiding the gap by stretching dates. Employment dates are verified.
  • A long emotional account.
  • Blaming others.
  • Sounding unsure whether you want to work.
  • Different explanations on the form and in the room.

Likely follow-up questions

  • Are you still preparing for those exams? Answer truthfully; an employer wants to know you will stay.
  • How have you kept your skills up to date? Name the course or work you did.
