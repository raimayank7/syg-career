Sales executive interview questions with answers

A sales interview is itself a sale: the product is you. Interviewers watch how you listen, how you handle a "no" and whether you speak in results. Expect questions about targets, a role play, and questions about how you find and keep customers.

What interviewers look for

  • Energy, clear speech and good listening.
  • Numbers: target against achievement, new accounts, collections.
  • Resilience: how you take rejection.
  • Honesty with customers; no over-promising.

12 questions with model answers

1. Sell me this pen.

Before I tell you about it, may I ask how often you write by hand, and for what? ... You sign documents every day. Then you need a pen that writes at once, without skipping, and looks right on a client's desk. This one has a smooth tip that does not blot, and a metal body. Would you like to try it on this paper? The point of the answer is to ask about the customer's need first and then match the product to it, instead of listing features.

2. How do you handle a customer who says your price is too high?

First I find out what he is comparing it with. Often the cheaper product is a different size or quality. Then I show the value: how long it lasts, the service, the scheme available. If price is truly the barrier, I see whether a smaller pack or different terms would suit him. I do not run down the competitor, and I do not promise a discount I am not authorised to give.

3. Tell me about a time you missed your target.

In the monsoon quarter last year I reached 86 percent. Two of my large outlets had closed for renovation. I told my manager early, listed fifteen outlets I had not yet opened in the same area, and visited them over the next month. Nine started ordering. The next quarter I closed at 108 percent. I learned not to depend on a few big accounts.

4. How do you find new customers?

I start with the area: I walk each beat and note every shop that stocks similar products but not ours. I ask existing retailers who else buys in the locality. For business customers I use references from satisfied clients. I keep a list, visit regularly, and note what each one said, so the next visit starts from there.

5. How do you deal with rejection?

It is part of the work. Most first visits end in "no", and I do not take it personally. I ask the reason, note it, and go back after some time with something new: a scheme, a sample, a smaller order. Many of my regular outlets said no the first two times.

6. How do you make sure payments come in on time?

I agree the credit period clearly at the time of the order and write it on the bill. I remind the customer a day or two before the due date, and I visit on the due date. I do not book fresh orders for an account that is long overdue without informing my manager. In my present job this brought the average delay down from 21 days to 9.

7. What would you do in your first month here?

Learn the products and prices thoroughly, travel the territory with the present executive or the manager, meet the main customers, and study the last year's sales by outlet. By the end of the month I would have a list of where the gaps are and a plan for the next quarter.

8. What motivates you in sales?

I like the clear scoreboard: the numbers show at the end of each day what I achieved. I also enjoy meeting people and the moment when a hesitant customer becomes a regular one.

9. How do you plan your day?

I fix the beat the evening before: which outlets, in what order, and what I want from each, whether an order, a payment or a display. I start early with the outlets that need the most time, keep the afternoon for collections and new calls, and update my report before I go home.

10. A customer complains about a defective product. What do you do?

I listen without arguing, look at the product, and apologise for the trouble. I explain the company's replacement process and start it the same day. Then I follow up until it is settled and inform him. A complaint handled well often makes a more loyal customer than one who never had a problem.

11. Why sales, and not another field?

I am comfortable talking to strangers, I do not mind travelling, and I like work where effort shows directly in results. During my summer internship I visited 120 outlets, and I found I enjoyed the days in the market more than the days in the office.

12. Are you comfortable with travel and field work?

Yes. I have a two-wheeler and a valid licence, and I am used to being in the market six days a week.

Model answers are examples. Use your own experience and figures, and for anything that changes (rates, limits, rules) check the official source before the interview.

How to prepare

  • Know your own numbers by heart: target, achievement, rank, new accounts.
  • Study the company's products, prices and main competitors before the interview.
  • Practise a role play aloud with a friend: ask about need, present, handle one objection, ask for the order.
  • Dress neatly; in sales, the interviewer imagines you standing before a customer.
