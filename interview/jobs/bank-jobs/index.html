Bank interview questions with answers (clerk and officer)

The interview for a bank clerk or officer post is a personal interview before a panel, usually fifteen to twenty minutes. Panels ask about your background, why you want to join a bank, basic banking and economic awareness, and how you would deal with customers. They look for clarity, honesty and a settled temperament.

What interviewers look for

  • Clear reasons for choosing banking.
  • Basic banking awareness and reading habit.
  • Courtesy and patience suited to customer service.
  • Readiness for transfers and rural service.

12 questions with model answers

1. Why do you want to join a bank?

A bank job combines three things I want: work with numbers and money, daily contact with people, and a stable career with a clear path of growth through internal examinations. My graduation in commerce gave me the base, and helping my family and neighbours with bank forms showed me how much a helpful bank employee matters to ordinary people.

2. You are an engineering graduate. Why banking?

Engineering trained me to work with systems and solve problems, and banks today run on technology. I found during my final year that I was more interested in finance and in work that involves people. I have prepared seriously for this examination for a year, and I intend to make my career here, not to treat it as a stop-gap.

3. What is the difference between a savings account and a current account?

A savings account is meant for individuals to save; it earns interest and may have limits on the number of transactions. A current account is meant for businesses with frequent transactions; it normally earns no interest and may allow an overdraft facility.

4. What is the repo rate?

It is the rate at which the Reserve Bank of India lends short-term funds to commercial banks against government securities. When the repo rate is raised, borrowing becomes costlier for banks, which tends to raise lending rates and cool inflation; when it is cut, the opposite. I would quote the current rate only after checking the Reserve Bank's latest policy announcement.

5. What is the difference between NEFT, RTGS and IMPS?

All three transfer money between bank accounts electronically. RTGS settles each transaction individually in real time and is meant for large-value transfers. NEFT settles transfers in batches and is used for ordinary amounts. IMPS is an instant transfer service available round the clock, commonly used from mobile and internet banking. The limits and charges are set by the regulator and banks and change from time to time.

6. What is a non-performing asset?

A loan becomes a non-performing asset when the borrower stops paying interest or instalments for a period laid down by the Reserve Bank, so that the loan no longer earns income for the bank. High non-performing assets reduce a bank's profit and its ability to lend.

7. What is KYC and why is it needed?

KYC stands for Know Your Customer. It is the process by which a bank verifies the identity and address of a customer using documents before opening an account, and updates it periodically. It helps prevent fraud, money laundering and misuse of accounts.

8. How would you deal with an elderly customer who cannot fill a form?

I would greet him, offer him a seat, and explain the form in his own language. I would help him fill it, read it back to him before he signs, and make sure he understands what will happen next. If the queue is long, I would ask a colleague to help or attend to him at a suitable counter, but I would not send him away.

9. Are you willing to work in a rural branch?

Yes. I understand that officers are posted where the bank needs them, and rural branches are where banking makes the most difference. I grew up in a small town and am comfortable with that life.

10. What is financial inclusion?

It means making basic financial services, such as a bank account, payments, credit and insurance, available and affordable to everyone, especially people in villages and low-income groups who were outside the banking system.

11. Tell us something about your home town or state.

I am from Alwar in Rajasthan, known for its fort and for the Sariska reserve nearby. The main occupations are agriculture and small industry, and many families depend on dairy. Knowing the local economy would help me as a banker there. Panels often ask this; prepare a few facts about your district's economy, crops and industry.

12. What did you read in today's newspaper?

Answer with one or two items you actually read that morning, preferably on the economy or banking, and be ready for a follow-up question. Read a newspaper daily for at least a month before the interview.

Model answers are examples. Use your own experience and figures, and for anything that changes (rates, limits, rules) check the official source before the interview.

How to prepare

  • Read the notification and know the bank: its headquarters, chairman, and recent developments, from the bank's own website.
  • Revise basic banking terms and follow the Reserve Bank's announcements for current figures; never quote a rate from memory.
  • Prepare your own background thoroughly: your degree subjects, your district, your hobbies.
  • Dress formally, greet the panel, and answer briefly and honestly. "I am sorry, I do not know" is acceptable.
