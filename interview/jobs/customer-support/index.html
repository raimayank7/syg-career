Customer support and BPO interview questions with answers

Support and BPO interviews test how you speak and listen more than what you know. There is usually a short conversation round, a typing or listening test, and sometimes a mock call. Clear, calm, complete sentences matter more than fancy words.

What interviewers look for

  • Clear speech at an even pace, in the language of the process.
  • Patience and empathy with an upset customer.
  • Willingness to work shifts and follow a process.
  • Basic computer and typing skill.

12 questions with model answers

1. Tell me about your day yesterday.

Yesterday I got up at six and went for a walk. After breakfast I spent two hours preparing for this interview: I read about your company and practised some answers. In the afternoon I helped my brother with his homework, and in the evening I went to the market with my mother. I slept by ten so that I would be fresh today. This question checks whether you can speak in simple, correct sentences in order; keep to the past tense and finish what you start.

2. How would you handle an angry customer?

I would let him speak without interrupting, because he first wants to be heard. Then I would apologise for the trouble and repeat the problem in my own words to show I have understood. I would tell him what I am going to do and how long it will take, do it, and confirm with him before closing. I would keep my voice calm throughout, even if his is not.

3. What is the difference between sympathy and empathy?

Sympathy is feeling sorry for someone. Empathy is understanding how the person feels from their side. In support work, empathy means saying, "I understand that it is frustrating to be charged twice; let me check this for you now," and then acting on it.

4. A customer asks for something that company policy does not allow. What do you do?

I would explain politely what I cannot do and why, in simple words, and then tell him what I can do instead. If he is still not satisfied, I would follow the process for escalation to a senior. I would not make a promise outside the policy just to end the call.

5. Are you comfortable with night shifts and rotational offs?

Yes. I understand that support runs round the clock. I have discussed it with my family and arranged my travel. I would like to know about the transport facility for night shifts.

6. Why do you want to work in customer support?

I like talking to people and solving their problems. In my part-time job at a mobile store I dealt with 25 to 30 customers a day, and the part I liked best was when a customer who came in upset left satisfied. I would like to do that as a career and grow into a team leader.

7. What would you do if you did not know the answer to a customer's question?

I would not guess. I would tell the customer that I want to give the correct information and ask for a moment to check, then look it up in the knowledge base or ask my senior. If it needs more time, I would arrange to call back and make sure the call is made.

8. Speak for one minute on your favourite film, or your city.

My city is Jaipur, known as the Pink City because of the colour of the old buildings. I like it for three reasons. First, its history: the forts and palaces are part of daily life, not only for tourists. Second, the food, especially the sweets. Third, the people, who are warm and helpful. If you visit, I would suggest going to the old market in the evening. A short structure, such as three reasons, keeps you from drying up.

9. How do you handle repetitive work?

I set small targets for myself, such as closing each call correctly the first time, and I remind myself that the customer is calling for the first time even if I have heard the problem fifty times. I also take my breaks properly so that I am fresh.

10. What is good customer service, in your view?

Solving the customer's problem correctly, the first time, politely and without making him repeat himself. And telling the truth, even when the answer is not what he hoped for.

11. How fast can you type, and how do you keep accuracy?

About 40 words a minute in English. I look at the screen, not the keys, and I read each entry once before submitting. For names and numbers I confirm with the customer by repeating them back.

12. Where do you see yourself in two years?

I would like to be a senior associate handling escalations or helping to train new joiners, and after that move towards a team leader role.

Model answers are examples. Use your own experience and figures, and for anything that changes (rates, limits, rules) check the official source before the interview.

How to prepare

  • Practise speaking aloud for one minute on simple topics every day for a week.
  • Take an online typing test and know your real speed.
  • Listen to how good service calls are opened and closed: greeting, name, confirming the problem, summary, thanks.
  • Sleep well before the interview; a tired voice shows.
