Mechanical engineer (fresher) interview questions with answers

For trainee posts in production, quality and maintenance, interviewers ask basic technical questions from your course, questions about your training or project, and questions about attitude: shifts, safety, working with operators. They prefer a candidate who knows a little thoroughly to one who has memorised definitions.

What interviewers look for

  • Basics of manufacturing, materials and measurement.
  • What you actually did in training and your project.
  • Safety awareness and discipline.
  • Willingness to work on the shop floor and in shifts.

12 questions with model answers

1. What is the least count of a vernier caliper and a micrometer?

Least count is the smallest measurement an instrument can read. A common vernier caliper has a least count of 0.02 mm, and a common micrometer 0.01 mm. I used both in my training to measure turned parts, and a bore gauge for inside diameters.

2. What is the difference between accuracy and precision?

Accuracy is how close a measurement is to the true value. Precision is how close repeated measurements are to each other. An instrument can be precise but not accurate if it has a constant error, which is why instruments are calibrated.

3. What is tolerance, and why is it given on a drawing?

Tolerance is the permitted variation in a dimension. No process can make every part exactly the same, so the drawing states the upper and lower limits within which the part will still fit and work. Tighter tolerance costs more to produce, so it is given only where needed.

4. What are the main types of fit?

Clearance fit, where the shaft is always smaller than the hole and can move freely. Interference fit, where the shaft is larger than the hole and must be pressed in. Transition fit, which may give a small clearance or a small interference depending on actual sizes.

5. What is the difference between a lathe and a milling machine?

On a lathe the workpiece rotates and a single-point tool is fed against it, producing cylindrical surfaces by turning, facing and threading. On a milling machine a multi-point cutter rotates and the workpiece is fed against it, producing flat surfaces, slots and profiles.

6. What is preventive maintenance?

It is maintenance done at planned intervals, such as cleaning, lubrication, inspection and replacing worn parts, to prevent breakdowns. Breakdown maintenance is done after a failure. Preventive maintenance costs less overall because it avoids unplanned stoppages. In my training I helped with weekly lubrication and belt checks on six machines.

7. What is 5S?

It is a workplace organisation method from Japan with five steps: sort out what is not needed, set in order what remains, shine or clean the workplace, standardise the method, and sustain the practice. It makes work safer and faster and makes abnormalities easy to see.

8. What is the difference between quality control and quality assurance?

Quality control is checking the product to find defects: inspection and testing. Quality assurance is building the process so that defects do not occur: standards, training, audits. Control is about the product; assurance is about the process.

9. Which safety rules must be followed on the shop floor?

Wear the required protective equipment: safety shoes, goggles, gloves or ear protection as the job needs. No loose clothing or jewellery near rotating machines. Never remove guards or bypass interlocks. Switch off and isolate a machine before maintenance. Keep gangways clear, know the emergency stops and exits, and report any unsafe condition at once.

10. Explain your project.

We designed and fabricated a pedal-operated hacksaw for workshops without reliable power. It uses a crank and slotted lever mechanism to convert the rotary motion of the pedals into the to-and-fro motion of the saw. I did the welding and assembly. In the demonstration it cut a 20 mm mild steel bar. If I did it again, I would add a flywheel for smoother cutting.

11. What is the difference between hardness and toughness?

Hardness is resistance to scratching or indentation. Toughness is the ability to absorb energy without breaking. Glass is hard but not tough; mild steel is tougher but less hard. Heat treatment is used to get the balance needed.

12. Are you ready to work in shifts and to work with your hands?

Yes. I chose mechanical engineering because I like machines. In training I worked alongside operators on the first shift, loading parts and checking the first piece. I understand that a trainee learns on the shop floor, and I am ready for rotational shifts.

Model answers are examples. Use your own experience and figures, and for anything that changes (rates, limits, rules) check the official source before the interview.

How to prepare

  • Revise your own course notes on manufacturing processes, metrology and materials; questions come from the basics.
  • Be able to draw and explain your project and what you personally did.
  • Remember the machines and instruments you used in training, by name.
  • If the company's products are known, read how such parts are made.
