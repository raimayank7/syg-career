HR executive and recruiter interview questions with answers

Candidates for HR roles are interviewed by people who interview for a living, so vague answers are noticed quickly. Expect questions on how you recruit, how you handle people problems, and what you know of HR processes, with requests for real examples.

What interviewers look for

  • Process knowledge: recruitment cycle, onboarding, records.
  • Judgement and discretion with confidential matters.
  • Numbers: positions closed, time to fill, joining ratio.
  • Clear, courteous communication.

12 questions with model answers

1. Walk me through your recruitment process.

I start by understanding the requirement with the hiring manager: duties, skills, experience, budget. I prepare the job description and post it on portals and our referral channel. I screen resumes against the must-have skills, call candidates for a short telephone round to check interest, communication and availability, and shortlist for the manager. I schedule interviews, collect feedback the same day, and for selected candidates I check documents and references before the offer. Then I stay in touch until the joining day.

2. How do you screen a resume?

I look first for the essential skills and experience in the job description, then at stability and gaps, and then at how results are described. I spend more time on the last two jobs. For a large number of applications I use the portal's filters and keywords to make a first cut, and I read the shortlisted ones fully.

3. What would you do if a selected candidate does not join?

Prevention first: I keep in touch during the notice period, share joining details early and involve the manager in a call. If the candidate still declines, I ask the reason, record it, and go at once to the second candidate, whom I would have kept informed. Over time I review the reasons to see whether our offer process needs to change.

4. How do you reduce the time taken to fill a position?

By getting a clear requirement at the start, keeping a bank of earlier shortlisted candidates, encouraging employee referrals, fixing interview slots in advance with managers, and giving feedback within a day. In my present company these steps brought the average from 35 days to 24.

5. What is attrition and how can HR help to control it?

Attrition is employees leaving the organisation over a period, usually expressed as a percentage of average headcount. HR can help by hiring people who fit the role, a proper induction, regular conversations with new joiners, fair and timely pay and appraisal, and exit interviews whose findings are actually acted on.

6. What does onboarding include?

Before joining: the offer and appointment letter, documents list and first-day details. On joining: collecting documents and forms, creating the employee record, issuing identity card and system access, explaining policies, and introducing the team. In the first month: role training and a check-in at the end of the first week and first month.

7. An employee complains to you about their manager. What do you do?

I listen in private and take notes, without taking sides. I ask what outcome the employee expects and explain what I can keep confidential and what I may have to share to act on it. Depending on the matter I would speak to the manager, involve my HR head, or follow the formal grievance procedure. For a complaint of harassment I would follow the company's policy and the law strictly, through the proper committee.

8. How do you maintain confidentiality?

I discuss employee matters only with those who need to know. I keep salary and personal records in restricted folders, do not leave papers or screens open, and never discuss one employee with another. If I am unsure whether to share something, I ask my HR head.

9. Which HR tools or software have you used?

Job portals for sourcing, an HR system for employee records, attendance and leave, and Excel for trackers and reports with pivot tables and VLOOKUP. I prepare a weekly hiring report for management.

10. How would you handle a manager who rejects every candidate?

I would sit with the manager and go through the last few rejections to find the common reason. Usually the requirement was not clear, or is unrealistic for the budget. We would then revise the job description or the range, and I might ask the manager to join the first screening call for a few candidates so that we are aligned.

11. What is the difference between recruitment and selection?

Recruitment is attracting and gathering a pool of suitable applicants. Selection is choosing the right person from that pool through screening, tests and interviews.

12. Why HR?

I studied psychology and enjoy understanding what makes people do good work. In my internship I found I liked the organised side too: records, follow-up, getting people in place on time. HR needs both, and that suits me.

Model answers are examples. Use your own experience and figures, and for anything that changes (rates, limits, rules) check the official source before the interview.

How to prepare

  • Know your figures: positions closed, average time to fill, offers against joinings.
  • Revise the stages of the employee life cycle and basic terms.
  • For labour law questions, state the principle and say you would check the current provision; do not guess figures.
  • Prepare two real examples: a difficult hire and a people issue you handled.
