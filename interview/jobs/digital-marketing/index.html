Digital marketing (fresher) interview questions with answers

Digital marketing interviews for junior roles test whether you understand the main channels, can think about a customer, and have actually tried something: a page, a blog, a small campaign. Tools change every year, so interviewers care more about sound reasoning and curiosity than about memorised features.

What interviewers look for

  • Understanding of the main channels and when to use each.
  • Evidence of practice: your own page, blog or project.
  • Comfort with numbers and simple analysis.
  • Clear writing.

12 questions with model answers

1. What is SEO, and what are on-page and off-page SEO?

Search engine optimisation is improving a website so that it appears higher in unpaid search results for relevant searches. On-page SEO is what you do on the site: useful content, clear titles and headings, descriptive links, fast loading, mobile-friendly pages. Off-page SEO is what happens elsewhere, mainly other good sites linking to yours and mentions of the brand.

2. What is the difference between SEO and paid search advertising?

SEO earns visits from unpaid results; it takes time and effort but each visit is not charged. Paid search places advertisements above or beside the results, charged usually per click; it starts at once and stops when the budget stops. Most businesses use both: paid for quick results, SEO for the long term.

3. What is a keyword, and how do you choose keywords?

A keyword is the word or phrase a person types into a search engine. I would list what our customers might search for, check search volume and competition in a keyword tool, and choose phrases that show clear intent and that we can realistically rank for. Longer, specific phrases usually bring fewer but more interested visitors.

4. Which metrics would you track for a campaign?

It depends on the goal. For awareness: reach and impressions. For traffic: clicks and click-through rate. For results: conversions, conversion rate and cost per conversion, and finally the revenue against the money spent. I would decide the one or two metrics that matter before the campaign starts.

5. What is click-through rate and conversion rate?

Click-through rate is clicks divided by impressions, as a percentage: how many of those who saw the advertisement clicked it. Conversion rate is conversions divided by clicks or visits: how many of those who came did what we wanted, such as filling a form or buying.

6. How would you promote a new local bakery online with a small budget?

First, the free basics: a complete business listing on maps with photos, timings and phone number, and a social media page with good pictures of the products. Then regular posts and short videos, and asking happy customers for reviews. With a small budget I would run advertisements only to people within a few kilometres, around festivals and weekends, with an offer, and measure calls and orders.

7. Which social media platform would you choose for a business?

The one where its customers are. A fashion or food brand selling to consumers usually does well on visual platforms; a business selling to other businesses does better on professional networks. I would look at who the customer is and what content the business can produce regularly, and start with one or two platforms done well.

8. What makes good content?

It answers a real question or need of the reader, is clear and specific, and is honest. For search it should cover the topic properly under a clear title; for social media it should catch attention in the first seconds. I always ask: would I read or share this if I were the customer?

9. What is A/B testing?

Showing two versions of something, such as an advertisement headline or a page, to similar audiences at the same time, changing only one thing, and seeing which performs better. It replaces opinion with evidence.

10. Tell me about something you have done yourself.

I manage the social media page of my cousin's boutique. In four months I took it from 300 to 1,900 followers by posting three times a week, using short videos of new arrivals and answering every message the same day. Enquiries on the page went from about five a week to about twenty. I also wrote a blog of eight posts on a free platform to learn the basics of SEO.

11. How do you keep up with changes in this field?

I follow the official blogs and help pages of the main advertising and search platforms, since rules change often, and one or two newsletters. I also try new features on my own small page before recommending them.

12. What is the difference between reach and impressions?

Reach is the number of different people who saw the content. Impressions is the number of times it was shown, including repeat views by the same person.

Model answers are examples. Use your own experience and figures, and for anything that changes (rates, limits, rules) check the official source before the interview.

How to prepare

  • Have something of your own to show: a page, a blog, a small campaign with numbers.
  • Look at the company's website and social media beforehand and come with two honest suggestions.
  • Revise the basic terms and formulas for rates.
  • For platform-specific rules, say you would check the platform's current help pages.
