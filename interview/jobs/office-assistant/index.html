Office assistant, data entry and receptionist interview questions

Interviews for office support jobs are short and practical. Expect a few questions about yourself, a typing or computer test, and questions on how you would handle everyday situations: a ringing phone, a visitor, a mistake in an entry. Employers are looking for someone reliable, neat and polite.

What interviewers look for

  • Punctuality and regular attendance.
  • Accurate typing and basic computer use.
  • Polite speech with visitors and on the phone.
  • Honesty and care with documents and cash.

12 questions with model answers

1. What computer work can you do?

I can type in English at 35 words a minute and in Hindi at 25. In Excel I can enter data, sort and filter, and use simple formulas such as SUM. In Word I can type and format letters. I can send email with attachments, scan and print documents, and search the internet. I completed a six month computer course last year.

2. How do you make sure your data entry is correct?

I read each entry from the paper once before typing and once after. I enter figures in batches and compare the total with the total on the source. If I am not sure of a word or number, I ask instead of guessing. At the end of the day I check a few entries at random.

3. How would you answer the office telephone?

I would pick up within three rings and say, "Good morning, [company name], Pooja speaking. How may I help you?" I would listen, note the caller's name, number and message, and repeat the number back. If the person asked for is not available, I would say so politely and promise to pass on the message, and then do it.

4. A visitor arrives without an appointment and insists on meeting the manager. What do you do?

I would greet him, ask his name and the purpose, and request him to be seated. I would inform the manager or his assistant by phone or message. If the manager cannot meet him, I would explain politely and offer to take a message or fix a time. I would not send him in without permission.

5. How do you keep files so that papers can be found quickly?

I keep a separate file for each subject or party, arrange papers by date with the latest on top, label the file clearly on the spine, and keep an index. On the computer I use clear folder and file names with dates. In my last job any delivery receipt could be found in under two minutes.

6. What will you do if you make a mistake?

I will correct it at once if I can, and tell my senior what happened, without hiding it. Then I will see how it happened and change my way of working so that it does not happen again.

7. Can you keep office matters confidential?

Yes. I will not discuss letters, salaries, accounts or conversations I see or hear at work with anyone outside, or with colleagues who do not need to know.

8. What would you do if two people gave you urgent work at the same time?

I would ask each how soon it is needed. If both are truly urgent and I cannot do both, I would tell them so and ask which should come first, or ask my supervisor to decide. I would not silently leave one undone.

9. Why should we give you this job?

I am regular, careful and willing to learn. I worked for a year in a transport office doing entries, filing and phone work, so I know what an office needs. I live nearby and can reach on time every day.

10. Are you ready to do other small tasks if needed?

Yes. In a small office everyone helps. I am comfortable with bank and courier work, photocopying, and keeping the stationery and stock register.

11. How do you deal with a rude caller?

I stay polite and do not answer in the same tone. I listen, note the problem, and tell the caller what I will do. If he is abusive, I would inform my senior.

12. What are your working hour expectations?

I am available for the office timings you follow, six days a week. If there is extra work on some days I can stay, with a little notice so that I can inform my family.

Model answers are examples. Use your own experience and figures, and for anything that changes (rates, limits, rules) check the official source before the interview.

How to prepare

  • Practise typing daily for a week before the test and know your true speed.
  • Practise a telephone greeting and a short self-introduction aloud.
  • Carry a neat file with your biodata or resume, certificates and photocopies.
  • Reach fifteen minutes early; for this kind of job, punctuality at the interview is itself the test.
