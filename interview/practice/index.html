Interview practice

A random question and a two minute timer. Answer aloud, then see how to answer. Nothing is recorded.

2:00

How to practise

  1. Choose a kind of question, or leave it on All.
  2. Read the question once, as you would hear it once in the room.
  3. Start the timer and answer aloud. Stand or sit as you would in the interview. Do not whisper, and do not just think the answer.
  4. Stop when you have finished, or when the two minutes end.
  5. Press "Show how to answer" and compare. Open the full page of model answers if you want more.
  6. Try the same question again before moving on, if the first attempt was weak.

Why speaking aloud matters

Reading model answers gives a pleasant feeling of being prepared. Then the interviewer asks the question, and the words do not come, or they come in the wrong order, or they go on for four minutes. Speaking is a physical skill, and it improves only by doing it. Ten questions answered aloud, badly at first, do more for you than a hundred answers read silently.

Getting more from each session

  • Record yourself with your phone's voice recorder and listen once. Notice speed, filler words and whether you answered the question in the first sentence.
  • Practise with a partner. Let a friend or family member read the question and ask one follow-up of their own. Follow-ups are where real interviews are decided.
  • Do five questions a day for a week, not thirty-five in one evening.
  • Keep a list of questions that troubled you and return to them.
  • Use your own facts every time. The tips show the shape; the content must be yours.

What a good answer sounds like

It answers the question at once, gives one real example or fact, links it to the job, and ends cleanly inside two minutes. If your answers regularly run past the bell, cut the background and keep the action and the result. If they finish in fifteen seconds, add the example. The STAR method page shows the proportions.

Questions people ask

Is my answer recorded?

No. The tool only shows a question and a timer. Nothing is recorded, stored or sent. If you want to hear yourself, use your phone's own voice recorder.

Why two minutes?

Most good interview answers take between one and two minutes. The timer trains you to finish within that.

How many questions are there?

The tool draws from 168 questions: the common questions and their follow-ups, behavioural questions, and the question sets for ten jobs.
