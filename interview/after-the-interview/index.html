After the interview: thank-you, follow-up and replies

The interview does not end at the door. A two-line thank-you, a patient follow-up and a gracious reply to whatever comes all add to the impression you leave.

The same day: write down the questions

Before anything else, while it is fresh, note every question you were asked, the ones you answered well and the ones you fumbled. This list is the most useful preparation you will ever have for your next interview, because the same questions return.

The thank-you message

If you have the interviewer's or HR contact's email, send a brief thank-you within a day. It is courteous, it reminds them of you, and few candidates do it. Keep it to four or five lines.

Subject: Thank you: interview for Accounts Assistant Dear Mr Sharma, Thank you for meeting me today for the Accounts Assistant position. I enjoyed learning about the work of your accounts team, especially the month-end closing process you described. The discussion has made me more interested in the role, and I am confident that my experience with Tally and GST entries would be useful to you. I look forward to hearing from you. With regards, Neha Kulkarni 98XXX XXX10

Mention one specific thing from the conversation, so that it is clearly not a form letter. Do not use the message to re-argue an answer or to ask about salary.

Waiting

If the interviewer told you when to expect a reply, wait until that date has passed. If nothing was said, a week to ten days is a reasonable time. Meanwhile, keep applying elsewhere; do not stop your search for one possibility.

The follow-up

Dear Ms Rao, I was interviewed for the Sales Executive position on 3 June. I remain very interested in the role and would be grateful for an update on the status of my application. Please let me know if you need any further information from me. Thank you, Vikram Singh Rathore

Send it once. If there is still no reply after another week, one more short message is acceptable. After that, leave it. Daily calls and messages do not help and can lose you a job you might have got.

If you receive an offer

  • Thank them, and ask for the offer in writing.
  • Read the letter fully: designation, place of posting, fixed and variable pay, deductions, working hours, probation, notice period, and any service agreement.
  • Ask about anything unclear before you accept.
  • It is reasonable to ask for a day or two to decide.
  • Accept in writing, and confirm your joining date.
  • Do not resign from a present job until you hold the written offer.

Dear Ms Rao, Thank you for the offer of the position of Sales Executive. I am happy to accept it on the terms set out in your letter dated 12 June, and I confirm that I will join on 1 July. Please let me know the documents I should bring on the first day. With regards, Vikram Singh Rathore

If you decline an offer

Do it promptly and politely, by phone or email, so that the employer can move to another candidate. A short reason is enough: "I have decided to accept another position that is closer to my family." Never simply fail to turn up on the joining day; the working world is smaller than it looks, and people remember.

If you are not selected

Reply with thanks; it costs nothing and leaves the door open.

Dear Mr Sharma, Thank you for letting me know, and for the opportunity to interview. I enjoyed meeting your team. If a suitable position comes up in future, I would be glad to be considered. If you are able to share any feedback on my interview, I would value it. With regards, Neha Kulkarni

Many employers will not give feedback, and you should not press. But some do, and candidates who replied graciously are sometimes called when the first choice does not join.

Learning from each interview

Look at your list of questions. For each one you answered poorly, write a better answer now and say it aloud. If the same kind of question has troubled you twice, such as a technical area or talking about a gap, give it an evening of proper preparation. Rejection is ordinary; most people are turned down many times before the right job. What matters is that each interview leaves you better prepared for the next.

A caution about fake offers

A real offer comes after a real interview, from the company's own official email address, and never asks you to pay a fee, deposit or charge of any kind. If an "offer" arrives for a job you did not apply for, or asks for money or bank details, treat it as a fraud and do not pay.

Questions people ask

Should I send a thank-you email after an interview?

Yes, within a day, if you have a contact address. Keep it to a few lines and mention one specific thing from the conversation.

How long should I wait before following up?

Until the date they gave has passed, or about a week to ten days if no date was given.

Should I resign as soon as I am told I am selected?

No. Wait until you have the offer in writing and have read its terms.
