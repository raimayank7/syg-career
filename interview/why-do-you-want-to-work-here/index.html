"Why do you want to work here?" How to answer

A question asked in almost every interview. Why it is asked, how to build your answer, model answers, and what to avoid.

Why interviewers ask it

This question separates candidates who applied to fifty companies without reading from those who chose this one. The interviewer wants to see that you know what the company does and have a reason for wanting this job in particular.

How to build your answer

  1. Research for half an hour: the company's website, what it makes or sells, where it operates, recent news, and the job description.
  2. Give one thing about the company that genuinely interests you.
  3. Give one thing about the role that fits what you want to do.
  4. Link both to your own skills or plans.
  5. Be truthful. If the honest reason is "a good local employer in my field", say that well.

Model answers

Fresher (manufacturing trainee)

I did my in-plant training in an auto components unit and liked shop-floor work. Your company makes precision machined parts for two-wheeler manufacturers, which is the kind of product I worked on, and from your website I saw that trainees rotate through production, quality and maintenance in the first year. That rotation is what I am looking for, because I want to learn the whole process before choosing an area.

Experienced (moving to a larger firm)

I have spent five years in a firm with one plant, and I have learned what I can there. Your company has four units and a shared finance team, so the accounts are more complex: inter-unit transfers, consolidation. That is the experience I want next. I also noticed you moved to a new ERP last year; I worked on a migration in my present job and would be glad to use that again.

Fresher (small local employer)

I live fifteen minutes away and have known this school since childhood; my cousin studied here. I have seen that it takes science exhibitions and activities seriously, which matches how I like to teach. I want to begin my teaching career in a school where I will be guided by senior teachers, and I believe I would get that here.

These are examples about invented people. Keep the structure, and put in your own facts and numbers.

What to avoid

  • "It is a reputed company." Say what, specifically.
  • Praising salary, timings or closeness to home as the only reason.
  • Getting the company's business wrong.
  • Flattery with no facts.
  • Saying you would take any job.

Likely follow-up questions

  • What do you know about our products? Know the main ones and who buys them.
  • Who are our competitors? Name one or two if you can; it shows you looked.
