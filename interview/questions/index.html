Common interview questions

12 questions that come up in nearly every interview, whatever the job. Each has its own page with model answers for freshers and experienced candidates.

  • Tell me about yourself. It is almost always the first question.
  • Why should we hire you? The interviewer is asking you to make their decision easy.
  • What are your strengths? They want to know whether you understand yourself and whether your strengths are the ones the job needs.
  • What is your biggest weakness? Nobody expects a candidate without weaknesses.
  • Why do you want to work here? This question separates candidates who applied to fifty companies without reading from those who chose this one.
  • Why are you leaving your current job? The interviewer wants to know whether the reason you are leaving will follow you into their company, and how you speak about people you have worked with.
  • Where do you see yourself in five years? They are checking three things: that you have thought about your career, that your direction fits what the company can offer, and that you intend to stay long enough to be worth training.
  • What are your salary expectations? The employer has a budget and wants to know whether you fit it.
  • Can you explain the gap in your career? A gap makes an interviewer wonder what happened and whether you are ready to work now.
  • Tell me about a challenge or difficult situation you faced. This is a behavioural question.
  • Do you have any questions for us? Almost every interview ends this way.
  • Are you willing to relocate, work in shifts or sign a service agreement? These are practical questions with real consequences.

Why the same questions come up everywhere

An interviewer has about half an hour to answer three things for themselves: can this person do the job, will they do it willingly, and will they fit with the people here. The common questions are simply the shortest routes to those answers. "Tell me about yourself" and "Why should we hire you?" are about ability. "Why this company?" and "Where do you see yourself in five years?" are about willingness and staying power. "Tell me about a challenge" and "What is your weakness?" are about how you behave when things are not easy.

Once you see what each question is really for, you stop looking for clever answers and start giving useful ones: a fact about yourself that helps the interviewer decide.

One method for all of them

  1. Answer the question that was asked, in the first sentence.
  2. Support it with one fact, number or short story from your own life.
  3. Connect it to the job you are being interviewed for.
  4. Stop after one to two minutes.

Every model answer on these pages follows that pattern. For questions that begin "tell me about a time when", the story is told with the STAR method.

How to use the model answers

Do not memorise them. An interviewer who hears a recited paragraph will ask a follow-up, and a borrowed answer has nothing behind it. Read an answer to see its shape, then write three or four notes of your own facts in the same order and practise saying them aloud. Each page lists the likely follow-up questions so that you can prepare those too.

Start with these three

If time is short, prepare tell me about yourself, why should we hire you and one story for tell me about a challenge. Between them they cover the opening, the main argument and the behavioural part of most interviews. Then add questions to ask at the end, because every interview finishes there.

Practise aloud with a timer

Model answers are examples. They show the shape of a good answer, reviewed in November 2026. Use your own facts: an answer copied word for word will not survive a follow-up question. Employers differ, and nothing here promises selection. Names in the examples are invented.
