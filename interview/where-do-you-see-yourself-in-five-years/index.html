"Where do you see yourself in five years?" How to answer

A question asked in almost every interview. Why it is asked, how to build your answer, model answers, and what to avoid.

Why interviewers ask it

They are checking three things: that you have thought about your career, that your direction fits what the company can offer, and that you intend to stay long enough to be worth training. They do not expect a prediction.

How to build your answer

  1. Describe a direction, not a job title: deeper skill, wider responsibility.
  2. Keep it realistic for the field and the company.
  3. Show that this job is a step on that path.
  4. Mention what you plan to learn.
  5. It is fine to say you cannot be precise about five years; then say what you are sure of.

Model answers

Fresher

In five years I would like to be an accountant who can handle the books independently up to finalisation, including GST and TDS. To get there I need the first two years to learn the daily work thoroughly, and I plan to study for a professional qualification alongside. I would like to grow within one organisation if I can.

Experienced

I see myself managing a sales area with a small team. For that I need to prove myself on a larger territory first, which is this role, and learn how to plan and review other people's work. If I do that well here, I hope the next step would come here too.

Technical

I want to be a strong back-end developer whom the team trusts with design decisions. In the next two years I want to own a service from design to production. Whether I later lead a team or stay technical, I will know better once I have done that; either way I want depth first.

These are examples about invented people. Keep the structure, and put in your own facts and numbers.

What to avoid

  • "In your chair." It is neither funny nor wise.
  • Naming a plan that takes you away: "I will start my own business", "I am preparing for government exams".
  • "I don't know."
  • An unrealistic jump: manager within a year.
  • A rehearsed speech about passion.

Likely follow-up questions

  • What are you doing to get there? Name a course, a skill or a habit.
  • Would you be willing to move to another city for growth? Answer honestly.
