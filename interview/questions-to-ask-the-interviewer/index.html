"Do you have any questions for us?" How to answer

A question asked in almost every interview. Why it is asked, how to build your answer, model answers, and what to avoid.

Why interviewers ask it

Almost every interview ends this way. "No, nothing" suggests you are not very interested. Two or three thoughtful questions show that you have considered the job seriously, and the answers help you decide whether you want it.

How to build your answer

  1. Prepare five questions; some will be answered during the interview, so you need spares.
  2. Ask about the work, the team, expectations and learning.
  3. Ask two or three, not ten. Watch the interviewer's time.
  4. Listen to the answers; a short follow-up shows you did.
  5. Leave pay, leave and timings for the HR discussion or the offer stage, unless they raise them.

Model answers

About the role

What would a typical day or week in this role look like? What are the most important things you would want the person to achieve in the first three months? Which part of the job do new joiners usually find hardest?

About the team

How large is the team, and whom would I report to? How does the team usually divide the work? How is work reviewed here: daily, weekly?

About learning and growth

What training do new employees receive? What has the usual path been for people who started in this role? Are there opportunities to work on other functions or projects over time?

About the company

What are the team's priorities for this year? I read that you opened a new unit recently; how does that affect this department? What do you enjoy most about working here?

About next steps

What are the next steps in the selection process, and when may I expect to hear from you? Is there anything in my background you would like me to clarify?

These are examples about invented people. Keep the structure, and put in your own facts and numbers.

What to avoid

  • "No, I have no questions."
  • Asking what the company does. You should already know.
  • Starting with salary, leave and working hours.
  • Questions already answered in the interview.
  • Challenging or personal questions to the interviewer.
  • A long list read from a notebook.

Likely follow-up questions

  • Is there anything else you would like to tell us? One sentence restating your interest and your main fit.
  • When can you join? Give an honest date, allowing for your notice period.
