Campus placement: a guide for freshers

Campus hiring is a sequence of filters. Each round tests one thing, and knowing what that thing is makes preparation much simpler.

The usual sequence

Companies differ, and you must read each company's own notice, but most campus drives follow some or all of these stages:

  1. Eligibility check: minimum marks through school and college, and sometimes no current backlogs. This is set by the company and applied before anything else.
  2. Pre-placement talk: the company presents itself and the role. Listen; the HR round will ask what you know.
  3. Written or online test: aptitude, and for technical roles, coding or subject questions.
  4. Group discussion (some companies).
  5. Technical interview: one or two rounds.
  6. HR interview.
  7. Offer, followed by documents and, later, joining formalities.

The aptitude test

It usually has three parts: quantitative (percentages, ratios, time and work, time and distance, profit and loss, averages), logical reasoning (series, arrangements, puzzles, coding and decoding) and verbal ability (reading comprehension, grammar, vocabulary). The topics are school-level; the difficulty is the clock.

  • Practise a little every day for two months, timed.
  • Learn to leave a hard question and come back; one stubborn question should not cost you three easy ones.
  • Check the marking scheme in the instructions; if there is negative marking, do not guess wildly.

The coding test (technical roles)

One to three problems to be solved in a language of your choice. Practise basic problems on arrays, strings, loops, recursion and simple data structures until you can write them without looking anything up. Read the problem fully, test with the sample inputs, and think about edge cases such as empty input.

The group discussion

A group of eight to twelve discusses a topic for ten to fifteen minutes while assessors watch. They are looking for clear thinking, listening and courtesy, not volume. See group discussion.

The technical interview

Expect three kinds of question:

  • Your project and internship, in detail. Know every part, including what your teammates did.
  • Core subjects from your course. Revise the fundamentals of two or three subjects you are strongest in and say so when asked "which subject do you like?".
  • Problem solving on paper or a shared screen. Think aloud.

Model answers for a software fresher are on the software developer page, and for core branches on the mechanical engineer page.

The HR interview

By this stage the company is inclined to hire you; the HR round checks attitude, communication and practical matters. Prepare tell me about yourself, why this company, strengths, weakness, five years from now, and the practical questions on relocation, shifts and service agreements. Answer those honestly.

Preparing through the final year

  • Six months before: start aptitude practice; choose and build one solid project.
  • Three months before: prepare your one-page resume; revise core subjects; begin mock interviews with friends.
  • One month before: timed mock tests weekly; practise speaking on general topics for group discussions; read a newspaper daily.
  • The week before each company: read about the company; revise from its job description.

On the day of the drive

  • Carry several copies of your resume, photographs, your college identity card and mark sheets.
  • Dress formally and eat before you go; drives run late into the evening.
  • Be patient in the waiting between rounds, and courteous to volunteers and staff.
  • Do not compare notes anxiously with others after each round. Stay with your own preparation.

If you are not selected

Most students are rejected by several companies before one says yes. After each drive, write down what was asked and where you fell short, and fix that one thing. If campus placement does not work out, off-campus applications, walk-in drives and smaller companies are real routes into the same careers; the preparation is the same.

A caution

A genuine employer does not ask a student to pay for a job, a "training deposit" or "registration". If someone asks for money in connection with placement, check with your college placement office before doing anything.

Questions people ask

What are the rounds in campus placement?

Usually an aptitude or online test, sometimes a group discussion, one or two technical interviews and an HR interview. Each company sets its own process.

How do I prepare for the aptitude test?

Practise quantitative, reasoning and verbal questions daily for about two months, against the clock.

What if I have a backlog?

Eligibility rules are set by each company. Read the notice; some require no current backlogs. Clear them as early as you can.
