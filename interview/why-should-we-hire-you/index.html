"Why should we hire you?" How to answer

A question asked in almost every interview. Why it is asked, how to build your answer, model answers, and what to avoid.

Why interviewers ask it

The interviewer is asking you to make their decision easy. They have a job with certain needs and several candidates. They want to hear, in your words, the match between what they need and what you bring, with evidence. It is not an invitation to boast or to plead.

How to build your answer

  1. Before the interview, pick the three main needs of the job from the advertisement.
  2. For each need, have one proof from your study or work.
  3. Say them plainly: "You need A; I have done A, for example...".
  4. Add one thing about your attitude that you can also prove: reliability, learning quickly.
  5. Close in one line. Do not compare yourself with other candidates; you do not know them.

Model answers

Fresher (customer support)

From the job description I understand you need someone who speaks clearly in English and Hindi, can type fast and is comfortable with shifts. I have worked weekends at a mobile store for over a year, attending 25 to 30 customers a day, so I am used to explaining things patiently and handling complaints. My typing speed is 40 words a minute. And I have already been working weekends and evenings, so shifts are not new to me. I learn quickly and I turn up; my store manager would confirm both.

Experienced (accountant)

You are looking for someone who can take the books to finalisation and handle GST and TDS without supervision. That is what I do now, for a unit with a turnover of about ₹40 crore. I close the books by the 7th of each month, file the returns myself and prepare the audit schedules; our last audit closed without a qualification. I would bring the same discipline here, and since your business is also manufacturing, I would not need time to understand the kind of entries involved.

Fresher with a modest record

I will be honest that I am not the candidate with the highest marks. What I can offer is that I finish what I start. I worked part time through my degree, completed a six month computer course alongside, and did not miss a deadline in either. For an office assistant role, I think being dependable, careful with entries and willing to learn matters most, and those are my strengths.

These are examples about invented people. Keep the structure, and put in your own facts and numbers.

What to avoid

  • "Because I need this job." The need is yours; the answer must be about theirs.
  • Empty adjectives: hardworking, sincere, passionate, with no example.
  • Running down other candidates.
  • Repeating "tell me about yourself" word for word.
  • Promising things you cannot do.

Likely follow-up questions

  • What makes you different from other candidates? Do not guess about others. Restate your strongest proof.
  • What will you do in your first month? Learn the systems, meet the team, take on defined tasks; show you have thought about the role.
