Science, Commerce or Arts: how to choose your stream

The stream you pick in Class 11 does not decide your life, but it does decide which doors stay open at 17. Here is a way to choose that does not depend on what the neighbours' children did.

What each stream is really like

Science

The heaviest load of the three. Physics, Chemistry and either Mathematics or Biology, each with practical work, and for many students entrance coaching on top. It keeps the most courses open: engineering, medicine, pure science, and nearly everything the other streams lead to as well. The price is two hard years. A student who struggles through Science with poor marks may end with fewer real choices than one who did well elsewhere.

Commerce

Accountancy, Business Studies and Economics, often with Mathematics as an option. The subjects are new to everyone in Class 11, so nobody starts behind. It leads naturally to B.Com, chartered accountancy and the related professional courses, management, economics, banking and law. Accountancy rewards regular practice more than last minute effort.

Arts or Humanities

History, Political Science, Geography, Economics, Psychology, Sociology, languages and more, in combinations that differ by school. It suits students who read and write well, and it leads to law, civil services, teaching, journalism, design, psychology, social work and many degree subjects. The old idea that Arts is for weak students is simply wrong; some of the hardest examinations in the country are cleared by Arts graduates.

Five questions to ask yourself

  1. Which subjects do I study without being pushed? Not the ones you score in because of tuition, but the ones you open by choice.
  2. What kind of work can I picture doing? Read two or three of our career pages and notice which one holds your attention.
  3. Does that work need a particular stream? Medicine needs Biology. Most engineering needs Mathematics. Law, civil services, management and teaching do not need any one stream.
  4. How do I cope with pressure? Be honest. Two years of work you can sustain beat two years of work that breaks you.
  5. What does my school offer? Subject combinations differ. Ask before admission whether the pair you want, such as Commerce with Mathematics or Arts with Psychology, is taught.

Mistakes families often make

  • Choosing by marks alone. High marks in Class 10 do not mean a child must take Science, and average marks do not rule it out.
  • Choosing for status. A stream chosen to impress relatives has to be studied by the child, every day, for two years.
  • Following friends. Friends change sections within a month. The subjects stay.
  • Thinking the choice is final. It is not. A Science student can move to commerce, law or design after Class 12. Moving the other way, into engineering or medicine, is the one that is closed.

If you truly cannot decide

Write down the two or three careers you like most and check which stream each needs. If any of them needs Science, and you can manage the load, Science keeps that door open. If none of them does, choose the stream whose subjects you would rather read, because marks in Class 12 will matter for admission whichever way you go.

Not continuing to Class 11

A polytechnic diploma or an ITI trade is a real choice, not a failure. It suits students who want to work with their hands and earn early. See ITI and polytechnic and after Class 10.

Questions people ask

Which stream has the most scope?

Every stream leads to good careers. Science keeps the widest set of courses open, but only helps if you can study it well. Scope depends more on what you do in the stream than on its name.

Can I change my stream after Class 11?

Schools sometimes allow a change in the first weeks of Class 11, and boards have their own rules. Ask the school early; a change later usually means repeating the year.

Is Mathematics needed in Commerce?

Not for B.Com or chartered accountancy. It is asked for by many economics honours courses and helps in management entrance tests, so take it if you can manage it.

Can an Arts student get a good job?

Yes. Law, civil services, teaching, design, journalism, psychology and management are all open, and government examinations for graduates accept any subject.
