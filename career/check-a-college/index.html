How to check a college before you take admission

A degree is only as good as the recognition behind it. Ten minutes on the right official websites, before the fee is paid, can save years.

Three things to check, not one

  1. The university. The degree is awarded by a university, not by the college. The university must be on the University Grants Commission's lists.
  2. The college. It must be affiliated to that university for the course you are joining. Affiliation is given course by course.
  3. The course. Professional courses need the approval of their own council as well.

Which body looks after which course

Regulators are renamed and merged from time to time. If a name here has changed, the education ministry's website will point to the body that replaced it.

How to do the check

  1. Ask the college, in writing or on its prospectus, for three things: the name of the university that awards the degree, the affiliation for your course, and the council approval for the current year.
  2. Open the official website of each body yourself. Type the address or reach it from a government portal; do not follow a link the college sends.
  3. Search the body's list for the exact name and place of the institution. Similar names are common, and sometimes deliberate.
  4. Check that the approval covers your course and your year of admission. Approval for one course does not cover another.
  5. Keep a screenshot or printout with the date.

Warning signs

  • A "university" that is not on any UGC list, or a degree offered by an institute that is not a university at all.
  • Pressure to pay today, a discount that ends tonight, or fees taken in cash without a proper receipt.
  • A promise of a degree without attending or without examinations.
  • A guaranteed job, written nowhere.
  • An agent who says the seat is "fixed" for a payment. Admission to regulated courses runs through published counselling.
  • Approval "in process" or "applied for". That means not approved.

Beyond recognition

Recognition tells you the degree is valid. It does not tell you the college is good. For that, visit if you can, sit in the library and laboratories, and talk to current students away from the office. Ask what last year's students are doing now, and ask to speak to one. Read the refund rules before paying, and keep every receipt.

Questions people ask

How do I know if a university is fake?

Check the University Grants Commission's lists of recognised universities. UGC also publishes a list of fake universities. If the name is not on the recognised lists, do not take admission.

Is AICTE approval enough for an engineering college?

No. The college must also be affiliated to a recognised university for that course, because the university awards the degree.

Are distance and online degrees valid?

Only from universities and for programmes approved for that mode. UGC publishes the approved list for each session; check it for your year of admission.

What should I do if I already joined an unrecognised college?

Collect all receipts and papers, ask the college in writing for its approval letters, and complain to the regulator for that course and to your state's higher education department. Consumer forums have also heard such cases.
