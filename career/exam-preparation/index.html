How to prepare for an entrance exam, with or without coaching

Every examination looks different, but the method that works is the same for all of them. It is not secret, and it does not have to be bought.

Step 1: read the official bulletin, all of it

The body that holds the examination publishes an information bulletin. It gives the syllabus, the pattern of the paper, the marking scheme, eligibility and dates. Read it before any book or video. A surprising number of candidates prepare for months without knowing whether wrong answers lose marks.

Step 2: get the past papers

Most examining bodies publish earlier papers, and they are the best guide there is. Go through the last several years and mark, topic by topic, where the questions come from. You will find that some chapters appear every year and others almost never. That list, made by you, is worth more than any "important topics" sheet.

Step 3: make a timetable you can keep

  • Count the weeks you have and divide the syllabus across them, leaving the last part free for revision and tests.
  • Plan by the week, not by the hour. Hours slip; a weekly target can be caught up.
  • Put the hardest subject in the part of the day when you are freshest.
  • Keep school or college work in the plan if you are still studying. Board marks and degree marks matter too.
  • Leave one half day a week empty. It will be used for what overflowed.

Step 4: study to recall, not to recognise

Reading a chapter again feels like progress because it looks familiar. The test is whether you can produce it with the book shut. After each topic, close the book and write what you remember, or solve problems without looking at solved examples. Make short notes in your own words; you will revise from these, not from the textbook.

Step 5: practise under real conditions

  • Take full length tests with a clock, in one sitting, at the same time of day as the real paper.
  • If the real examination is on a computer, practise on a computer. Many bodies offer a free practice test on their website.
  • After each test, sort your mistakes: did not know it, knew it but slipped, or ran out of time. Each kind needs a different cure.
  • Keep an error notebook and read it before every test.

Step 6: revise more than once

What is studied once is mostly forgotten in a month. Plan to meet every topic at least three times: when you first learn it, a few weeks later, and in the last stretch.

What coaching does and does not do

Coaching gives a timetable, a peer group, teachers to ask and regular tests. Those are real benefits, most of all for a student who finds it hard to plan alone. It cannot do the practice for you, and it is not required by any examination. If you join one, ask to attend a class first, ask how many students are in a batch, and read the refund terms. Be wary of any institute that advertises results it cannot show.

If you prepare alone, you need three things that coaching would have supplied: a plan, a test series or the past papers used as tests, and one person, a teacher, senior or friend, to whom you can take doubts.

The last two weeks

  • No new topics. Revise notes and the error notebook.
  • Take a few timed papers, then stop two days before.
  • Print the admit card, read its instructions, and find out how long it takes to reach the centre.
  • Sleep at normal hours. A tired mind loses more marks than one missed chapter.

Questions people ask

Can I clear a competitive exam without coaching?

Yes. People do it every year for every examination. The syllabus and past papers are public. What you must supply yourself is a plan, regular timed practice and someone to ask when stuck.

How many hours a day should I study?

There is no right number. Steady, focused hours that you can repeat for months matter more than long days that burn you out. Measure the week by topics finished and tests taken, not by hours sat.

When should I start mock tests?

Start topic tests as soon as you finish topics, and full length tests once most of the syllabus is done. Do not wait until everything is perfect; the tests show what to fix.

Where do I find past papers?

On the official website of the examining body, usually under previous question papers or archive. Use these before any paid material.
