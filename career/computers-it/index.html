An IT career without engineering

You do not need a B.Tech to work in software. You do need to be able to show what you can build.

What the work is

IT work covers writing software, testing it, running servers and networks, supporting users, analysing data and designing screens. Much of it can be learned by doing.

The path, step by step

  1. Class 12 in any stream for many BCA courses; some universities ask for mathematics.
  2. A three year degree: BCA, or B.Sc in Computer Science or Information Technology. A four year honours degree is offered in many universities.
  3. Optional: MCA, two years, for a stronger technical base. Many universities admit through NIMCET or their own test.
  4. While studying: learn one programming language well, build small projects, and put them where people can see them.

Examinations and bodies

Examination or bodyWhat it is for
CUETUsed by central and many other universities for degree admission.
NIMCETFor MCA at the NITs.

How long it takes

Three to four years after 12th. Five with MCA.

Honest notes

  • A degree gets your application read. Projects and problem solving get you hired.
  • Avoid institutes that promise a "guaranteed job" for a fee. Free and low cost material from good sources teaches the same skills.
  • The first job may be in support or testing. Many people move to development from there.
  • English reading and writing matter in this field more than students expect.

Is this path for you?

It may suit you if:

  • You like building small things on a computer and fixing them when they break.
  • You can teach yourself from documentation and practice.
  • You are fine with skills going out of date and learning the next ones.

Think again if:

  • You expect the degree alone to bring a job. In this field, what you can show matters more.
  • You dislike sitting and concentrating for hours.

If the main road does not open

There is no single gate here, which is the good news. If one college or company says no, the answer is more work you can show: small projects, contributions to shared code, a support or testing job as a first step. Many people move into software from other degrees by this road.

Where to check

  • Your university's admission page
  • The NIMCET website of the conducting NIT
  • NPTEL and SWAYAM for free courses from IITs and other institutes
