Nursing and allied health careers

Hospitals run on many more people than doctors. These courses are shorter than MBBS and lead to steady, useful work.

What the work is

Nurses care for patients round the clock and carry out treatment. Pharmacists handle medicines. Physiotherapists help people move again. Technologists run laboratory tests, X-ray and scan machines, and operation theatre equipment.

The path, step by step

  1. Class 12, usually in Science with Biology. Some diploma courses accept other streams.
  2. Choose the course: B.Sc Nursing (four years), GNM (general nursing and midwifery diploma), B.Pharm (four years), D.Pharm (two years), BPT physiotherapy (four and a half years), or a B.Sc in an allied health subject such as medical laboratory technology or radiology.
  3. Admission is through NEET UG in some states and institutes, a state or university entrance test in others, or 12th marks. Check the rule for the college you want.
  4. Register with the council for your profession after the course, where one exists.

Examinations and bodies

Examination or bodyWhat it is for
NEET UGUsed for B.Sc Nursing admission in many government institutes and states.
State and university testsMany states hold their own test for nursing, pharmacy and paramedical courses.

How long it takes

Two to four and a half years after 12th, depending on the course.

Honest notes

  • Check recognition carefully: the nursing council for nursing, the Pharmacy Council of India for pharmacy. Many unrecognised institutes sell worthless certificates in this field.
  • The work involves shifts, nights and standing for long hours. Visit a hospital ward before you decide.
  • Indian nurses and therapists are in demand abroad, but each country has its own licensing exam and language test.

Is this path for you?

It may suit you if:

  • You are patient with people who are frightened, in pain or slow.
  • You can do careful, repeated work without cutting corners.
  • Shift work, including nights, is something you can live with.

Think again if:

  • You are looking for a desk job with fixed hours.
  • You see it only as a second prize after missing MBBS. The work deserves better, and so do you.

If the main road does not open

These courses differ a great deal from college to college. If one college does not admit you, another often will, so the real task is to check recognition with the council for that profession and to ask where recent students did their hospital training. A diploma can be the first step, with a degree added later while working.

Where to check

  • The Indian Nursing Council and your state nursing council
  • The Pharmacy Council of India
  • Your state's directorate of medical education
